The project should now be frozen as a complete B2B dairy commerce + retailer management platform, rather than continuing to add features informally.

Below is the master product definition I would use before development starts.

YOG MILK — MASTER PROJECT BLUEPRINT

1. What are we actually building?

Yog Milk is a B2B dairy ordering and retailer management platform.

It has two primary applications:

1. Retailer App

Used by dairy shops/retailers to:

Browse Yog Milk products 

Place normal and bulk orders 

Quickly reorder previous purchases 

Use available credit

2. Core philosophy of Yog Milk

The system should follow these principles from day one.

Principle 1 — B2B first

This is not Amazon for milk.

The system is designed around recurring business purchases by retailers.

Therefore:

Bulk quantities 

Repeat orders 

Retailer-specific pricing 

Credit 

Outstanding payments 

Delivery management 

Purchase history 

are more important than consumer-style shopping features.

Principle 2 — Every transaction must be traceable

Whenever something important happens, the system should record it.

For example:

Retailer placed order → Admin confirmed → Credit applied → Delivery assigned → Order delivered → Payment received.

Nothing important should simply disappear.

Principle 3 — Financial data must be accurate

Credit and payments are sensitive.

We should never calculate financial history only from the current balance.

Instead:

Transaction

      ↓

Ledger

      ↓

Current Balance

      ↓

Analytics

Credit, payments and outstanding amounts should have proper transaction records.

Principle 4 — Admin controls business rules

The app shouldn't hard-code business decisions wherever possible.

Admin should control things such as:

Product availability 

Product prices 

Retailer pricing 

Credit eligibility 

Credit percentage 

Credit expiry 

Payment terms 

Order status 

Delivery assignment 

Notification settings 

Principle 5 — Automation handles repetitive work

The system should automatically handle:

Credit allocation 

Credit expiry 

Payment deadlines 

Payment reminders 

Overdue alerts 

Order notifications 

Delivery notifications 

Credit notifications 

Analytics calculations 

Admin shouldn't have to manually check every retailer.

10. Credit system

This is one of Yog Milk's defining features.

Credit allocation

Admin defines a percentage for a retailer.

Example:

Credit Percentage = 10%

If an eligible completed purchase is:

₹20,000

then:

₹20,000 × 10%

= ₹2,000 credit

That allocation receives an expiry date.

11. Credit is not just a balance

We should maintain:

Credit Account

      │

      ├── Allocations

      ├── Usage

      ├── Expiry

      ├── Adjustments

      └── Reversals

Example:

Allocated       +₹2,000

Used            -₹800

Adjustment      +₹500

Expired         -₹300

────────────────────

Remaining       ₹1,400

The admin can therefore reconstruct the complete credit history.

12. Monthly credit expiry

Every allocation needs:

allocated_at

status

Used:

₹1,200

Remaining:

₹800

At expiry:

₹800 → EXPIRED

It cannot subsequently be used.

13. Seven-day credit payment

The retailer sees:

43. What makes this project professional

The professional aspect isn't simply having many screens.

It comes from the fact that the system has:

Transactional integrity

→ Orders, payments and credit are connected correctly.

Financial traceability

→ Every credit/payment movement has a record.

Operational visibility

→ Admin knows what's happening.

Retailer intelligence

→ Admin understands each retailer's purchasing behavior.

Automation

→ Reminders and expiry don't require manual work.

Security

→ Retailers cannot access other retailers' data.

Auditability

→ Administrative changes are recorded.

Scalability

→ The architecture can support more retailers/products later.

44. Final product definition

I would officially define Yog Milk as:

Yog Milk is a B2B dairy commerce and retailer management platform that enables retailers to purchase dairy products in bulk, reorder frequently purchased products, use admin-defined monthly credit, manage payments and outstanding balances, track deliveries and invoices, and monitor their purchasing history. The admin platform provides centralized control over retailers, products, pricing, orders, payments, credit, deliveries, notifications, analytics, reports, and audit history.

46. The final development rule

There should be one very important rule for the entire project:

Do not add a feature merely because it looks useful. Every feature must have a defined business purpose, database representation, workflow, permission model, notification behavior, and audit/history behavior where applicable.

That will prevent Yog Milk from becoming a collection of disconnected Flutter screens.

The end result should feel like a real B2B business platform, not a college-style shopping application.
